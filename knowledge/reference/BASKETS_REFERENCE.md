# Espresso Filter Baskets — Deep Reference

Detailed basket specifications, comparisons, and geometry. For the quick-reference version used during dialing-in, see `knowledge/BASKETS.md`.

---

## Why Baskets Matter

The basket is the last thing water passes through before becoming espresso. Its hole pattern determines how evenly water flows through the coffee puck — and uneven flow is the root cause of channeling, the single biggest extraction problem in espresso.

**Stock baskets** (the ones that ship with machines like the Gaggia Classic Pro) have inconsistent hole sizes, irregular spacing, and rough edges. Water finds paths of least resistance through larger holes, over-extracting some areas while under-extracting others.

**Precision baskets** (IMS, VST) use laser-cut or chemically etched holes with tight tolerances, creating more uniform flow distribution across the entire puck.

## Basket Types

### Stock Gaggia Baskets

The baskets included with the Gaggia Classic Pro are functional but imprecise. Hole sizes vary significantly, and the pattern isn't optimized for uniform extraction. Upgrading to a precision basket is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost improvements you can make.

### IMS Baristapro

IMS (Industria Materiali Stampati) is an Italian manufacturer specializing in precision filter baskets.

**Baristapro specifications (58mm, all double sizes):**

| Size | Height | Holes | Hole Diameter | Perforated Area | Material |
|------|--------|-------|---------------|-----------------|----------|
| 15g | 22mm | 661 | 0.30mm | 44mm | AISI 304 stainless steel |
| 18g | 24mm | 661 | 0.30mm | 44mm | AISI 304 stainless steel |
| 20g | 26mm | 661 | 0.30mm | 44mm | AISI 304 stainless steel |
| 22g | 28mm | 661 | 0.30mm | 44mm | AISI 304 stainless steel |

**Key characteristics:**
- Same hole count (661) across all sizes — capacity differences come from basket depth, not hole pattern
- Smaller holes (0.30mm) than VST — restricts flow slightly, requiring ~1-1.5 notches finer grind than VST
- Produces slightly more sweetness and delicacy in the cup compared to VST (Perfect Daily Grind testing)
- Edge diameter: 70mm (standard 58mm grouphead fit)

#### Nanotech Coating

The Nanotech variant adds a food-safe coating (quartz-based) to the basket surface. Benefits:

- **Reduced coffee oil adhesion** — easier to clean, less residue buildup between shots
- **Smoother surface** — reduces friction points that could cause uneven flow
- **No flavor impact** — the coating doesn't affect extraction chemistry, just maintenance
- **Longevity** — coating lasts years under normal use; no special care needed

The Nanotech coating is a convenience upgrade, not an extraction upgrade. If you're choosing between standard IMS Baristapro and Nanotech, the Nanotech is worth the small price premium for easier cleaning.

### VST Precision Baskets

VST (Vince Fedele's company) pioneered the precision espresso basket concept.

**Key specifications:**
- Available in 7g, 15g, 18g, 20g, 22g, 25g sizes
- Larger holes than IMS — allows higher flow rates
- Hole pattern centered to ±1.0mm, calibrated to ±5% total open area
- Strict dosing tolerance: ±1g (e.g., 18g basket = 17-19g dose)
- 25g model: 727 holes, 30mm height

**Key characteristics:**
- Flows faster than IMS at the same grind — requires slightly coarser grind for the same shot time
- Higher flow = potentially higher extraction yields (more water volume through the puck per unit time)
- Tighter dose tolerance window than IMS — more sensitive to over/under-dosing

### IMS vs VST: Which Is Better?

Neither. They're different design philosophies with marginal differences in the cup.

| Factor | IMS Baristapro | VST |
|--------|---------------|-----|
| **Hole diameter** | 0.30mm (smaller) | Larger (varies by size) |
| **Flow rate** | Slightly slower | Slightly faster |
| **Grind adjustment** | ~1-1.5 notches finer than VST | Reference point |
| **Dose tolerance** | Wider range | ±1g (stricter) |
| **Taste difference** | Slight edge in sweetness/delicacy | Slight edge in clarity |
| **In milk drinks** | Difference undetectable | Difference undetectable |

**Bottom line (from Perfect Daily Grind testing):** IMS had a slight edge in blind taste tests, but the difference was much smaller than the difference between *either* precision basket and a stock basket. Upgrade from stock to precision — which brand matters far less.

**From data analysis (Robert McKeon Aloe):** When the profile is well-optimized, basket differences become statistically insignificant. Machine-basket interaction also matters — a basket that performs well on one machine may not on another.

## Ridged vs Ridgeless

All precision baskets come in two variants: ridged (with a small lip near the top) and ridgeless (smooth walls).

| Factor | Ridged | Ridgeless |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| **Seal** | Ridge creates a mechanical lock in the portafilter — reliable seal | Relies on friction fit — can occasionally spin in the portafilter |
| **Removal** | Harder to pop out for cleaning | Easy to remove with a light push from below |
| **Puck prep** | No difference | No difference |
| **Extraction** | No difference | No difference |
| **Compatibility** | Works in all 58mm portafilters | Works in all 58mm portafilters |

**Recommendation:** Ridgeless is generally preferred for home use because it's easier to clean. The seal difference is negligible — a ridgeless basket held in by a backflush spring or rubber gasket won't move during extraction.

## Wall Angle (Taper)

Baskets taper slightly from top to bottom. This taper affects:
- **Puck expansion** — grounds absorb water and swell; a straight-walled basket would bind against the walls
- **Puck removal** — taper makes it easier to knock out the puck after extraction
- **Edge channeling** — if the taper is too aggressive, the puck can separate from the walls at the bottom, creating channels around the edges

Precision baskets (IMS, VST) have optimized taper angles. This isn't something you need to adjust — just know that it's one reason precision baskets extract more evenly than stock baskets.
